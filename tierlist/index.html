Blood Wave Necromancer

one-button blood caster
Necromancerunchanged

The Necromancer's carry build this season, and the most genuinely one-button build in the repo. Lord of Hatred's Hematolagnia key passive converts Blood Wave from an Ultimate into a Core skill - no cooldown, an Essence cost instead - and Kessime's Legacy then fires two waves per cast from both sides while pulling monsters in, so the old orb-pickup-and-reset gameplay is gone entirely. The multiplier engine is Overpower: you dump stacks with Blood Lance and regain them casting Blood Wave, which re-triggers the Bloodbath legendary node and the Dominate glyph. Cross-checked, and the sources genuinely disagree about how good that is: Maxroll's overall endgame list rates Blood Wave A, Maxroll's push list rates it A, Icy Veins rates it S, and TierListHQ's fresher 3.1.2 snapshot rates the class A with the comment that Blood Wave 'overperforms' but has 'limited depth behind it'. Two honest warnings. First, resource: the guide admits 'early on resource management can be an issue since Blood Wave as a core skill is very resource hungry', which is why the affix priorities below are dominated by Essence Regeneration and Resource Cost Reduction until Ring of Starless Skies lands. Second, and more serious, the endgame survivability package leans on a bug the guide states outright - 'Embattled Aspect makes your fortify healing instant (it is a bug)' - and pairs it with Endurant Faith so a finished character 'can feel quite immortal'. Treat that tech as volatile: it is the obvious patch target, and the build is still fine without it, just squishier. The guide author lists no cons beyond early gearing, which is worth reading as enthusiasm rather than as evidence.

Shield Charge Thorns Paladin

block-and-thorns melee bruiser
Paladinunchanged

The strongest solo Paladin build available this season, which is a smaller claim than it sounds and the summary says so on purpose. The damage is Physical Thorns rather than weapon hits: 'this build is using Shield Charge to deal Physical Thorns damage to your enemies through direct hits and Retribution pulsing around you everytime you block'. Shield Charge is the engine because its short-channel variant carries the Hit Count As Blocking modifier, so driving through a pack counts as blocks and pulses Retribution - which makes positioning the actual skill of the build, 'maximize the amount of enemies you hit with it by positioning accordingly'. Around it, Clash must be recast at least once every six seconds for Retribution chance, Thorns and Block Chance; Condemn gathers and applies Vulnerable, Weaken and Unhindered; Fortress is a three-second immunity that also converts Resolve into a large damage burst; and the two auras supply crit chance, Fortify, Resolve generation and Unstoppable. Everything scales through Resolve, capped at exactly 30 by three greater +6 Maximum Resolve tempers plus Aspect of Glynn's Anvil and the Phoba of Righteous Will charm set. Standing, honestly: Maxroll's overall endgame list rates this A and it is the best-rated Paladin build there, with five Paladin builds in C and two in D behind it; Maxroll's push list also rates it A. TierListHQ rates the class B, recording that the 3.1.0 launch buffs 'didn't translate to Pit performance', and the only S-tier Paladin entry anywhere is an explicitly group-only support build. Icy Veins dissents hard with two S-tier Paladin builds, but its list is dated 2026-07-04, before 3.1.1 and 3.1.2, and it is the season's known outlier. The guide's own cons are the right expectation-setter: short range, and requires good positioning - a melee thorns build has to physically drive through packs, which is exactly what a Pit boss floor punishes.

Ring of Fire Firewall Sorcerer

stationary damage-over-time caster
Sorcererunchanged

Sorcerer is a one-build class this season and this is the build. All of the damage is Firewall's damage-over-time and almost none of it comes from casting Firewall: the Ring of Fire variant means every *other* spell you cast applies the DoT while you stand inside the ring, which the guide describes as 'a rather peculiar build where you invest everything into Firewall but you almost never cast it'. Charged Bolts fires up to ten bolts per cast and each one applies a stack, while the Berú of the Threefold set auto-casts Meteor, Blizzard and Ball Lightning to refresh the ring and pile on hits - and the real skill of the build is staggering those three elemental procs into a two-second cadence so a Ring of Fire is always under you. The standing is genuinely split and the split is the point: Maxroll's push list rates Firewall S, the only Sorcerer build in S, while Maxroll's own overall endgame list keeps it at A and TierListHQ rates the class A. Maxroll's week-1 article reconciles them exactly - Firewall 'outperforms every other build by at least one order of magnitude' in raw DPS 'but is slow and clunky'. The guide's own cons box says the same in fewer words: very tanky, excellent AoE, good damage, against tricky to play, depends on a set, can't go very fast. The structural weakness is unfixable rather than a gearing problem: 'the moment you leave the Ring of Fire you suddenly deal no damage'. Nothing else the class offers comes close - Hydra, Frozen Orb and Meteor are D tier on both Maxroll lists - so read this as one exceptional build on a mid-tier class, not as a strong class.

Evade Counterswarm Spiritborn

mobility-driven poison swarm
Spiritbornunchanged

The best speed-farming build in the game this season and the only non-Barbarian build Maxroll rates S on both its overall endgame list and its push list. The name is slightly misleading in a way worth fixing up front: the damage is not the Evade. It comes from Pestilent Swarms created by Counterattack running the Lord of Hatred variant Counterattack of the Swarm, and Rushing Claw - with the Evasive Swipe variant, which dashes without needing a target - is used purely as a movement skill. You hold it down and the swarms orbiting you grind through everything you pass. The guide is explicit that Rushing Claw 'doesn't actually count as an Evade, so you can't use Cem', and the whole gearing problem follows from that: every cooldown affix on rings, gloves, helm and amulet exists to shorten Rushing Claw's charge cooldown. Two hard prerequisites: Ring of Writhing Moon, to keep the swarms circling you, and the Prodigy's Tempo paragon board and its legendary node. Because the damage is a damage-over-time, critical strikes cannot crit it, so crit chance is carried only far enough to proc Widow's Web - a genuinely inverted stat priority worth knowing before you gear. The honest counterweight to the S ratings: TierListHQ holds the class at A, 'great at speed, weak at bossing', and this build only partly answers that through Scourge triggering the five-piece set on single targets. And three separate bug situations touch it, which must not be run together. The build deliberately uses one - 'you use Tzic for the 6x damage bug on unstoppable enemies and bosses'. A second constrains it: a Spirit tag inheritance bug means charm +Ranks do not work through Lord of Hatred skill variants, which is why the Berú of Kwatli's Grace set is unusable in most variants; the developers have acknowledged it and a fix missed the Season 14 launch. A third makes Harmony of Ebewaka occasionally drop Ravager below its four-rank minimum. Separately, the Poison Stinger bug in Maxroll's week-1 article belongs to a different Spiritborn build and is not this one's.

Apocalypse Warlock

stack-and-detonate ultimate caster
Warlockunchanged

The Warlock's best pushing build, on the class every fresh source ranks last. The damage is a single screen-wide Annihilation detonation: 'this build uses Umbral Chains - Chain Whips to quickly build Apocalypse stacks to unleash a massive Annihilation explosion every few seconds, evaporating entire screens at once'. Annihilation normally carries a long Ultimate cooldown, and Chain Whips feeds stacks fast enough to circumvent it, so the Ultimate becomes a rhythm rather than a cooldown. Three scaling layers stack on top: Fire and Hellfire multipliers, an Overpower package built from the Ritualist Shard, Command Valloch and Tidal Aspect, and at the top end the Rite of the Nameless charm set, which pays out for casting Sigil skills - and once Fleshwrit Carapace gives Apocalypse the Sigil tag, the Ultimate feeds that set itself. Standing, stated without inflation: Maxroll's overall endgame list rates this B; Maxroll's push list rates it A, where Apocalypse and Lunatic are the two best Warlock entries. TierListHQ rates the class B and last, summarising the season as the largest buff pass and still last, 'fun does not equal meta'. Icy Veins lists three S-tier Warlock builds but that list predates 3.1.1 and is the season's widest source disagreement, so nothing here rests on it. Maxroll's own cons box is the honest expectation: combo build, damage ramping, mediocre survivability. The survivability problem is partly self-inflicted and you should plan for it before you gear - Scorching Fragment burns you when you attack, which is why the guide opens its equipment section insisting you carry healing via Undying Aspect, a transfigured copy on the amulet, or a large Life on Hit weapon roll. One staleness note the guide does not make itself: it was last updated 2026-06-30, on patch 3.1.0, and has not been revised for 3.1.1 or 3.1.2.
