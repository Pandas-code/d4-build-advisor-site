How to run them

Not constructed - this is what Maxroll's live Flurry Rogue guide runs, which is a useful anchor for a build that is otherwise speculative. Hire Subo and take Seeker and Opening Fire; Opening Fire's +25% Critical Strike Damage is straightforwardly good on a build that lands a great many small hits. Reinforce with Raheir and pick Provoke. Icy Veins' Flurry guide independently reaches the same hire with a Bastion reinforcement instead - between Provoke and Bastion, take Bastion if you are dying and Provoke if you are struggling to keep a pack together. Note that this is the opposite arrangement from the Penetrating Shot builds, both of which reinforce with Varyana for attack speed; the reasoning is that a melee Rogue's problem is staying alive in the pack, not attacking faster.

Constructed, and constrained by the same gap as every Rogue build on this site: no Rogue guide publishes War Plan picks, and Maxroll's Penetrating Shot planner has all seven activity trees empty on all eleven profiles. Summary

UNTESTED - original theorycraft, ranked by nobody in this form. Every published Rogue build this season is a bow build; the melee dagger side of the class is sitting at B tier on both Maxroll lists. But 3.1.x quietly stacked three buffs onto exactly that side: Flurry's damage went 140% to 196% (a 40% increase), the Way of the Blurring Blade talisman went 45% to 60% on its 2-piece and 200% to 250% on its 5-piece, and The Maestro went 24-30% to 45-60%. Meanwhile the bow builds' best-in-slot helm, Harlequin Crest, lost its Damage Reduction - which hurts a close-range build less than it hurts nothing, but means the defensive gap between melee and ranged narrowed from the ranged side. The thesis is that the tier lists have not caught up with a set of buffs that all landed on one archetype. I am openly sceptical of my own idea: Flurry sits in B tier on both Maxroll lists and the tier lists were updated after these buffs, which is real evidence against.

Reasoning

HypothesisFlurry's 3.1.x base damage increase from 140% to 196% and the Way of the Blurring Blade talisman's simultaneous 2-piece (45% to 60%) and 5-piece (200% to 250%) buffs are multiplicative with each other, so the combined effect on a full 5-piece Blurring Blade Flurry build is substantially larger than any single line item suggests - large enough that a melee dagger Rogue should be competitive with the B-tier bow builds and possibly with the A-tier ones, despite no tier list having moved it.
Confidencelow
How to test itFirst check the arithmetic in-game before investing: confirm whether the Way of the Blurring Blade 2-piece and 5-piece bonuses actually multiply with each other and with Flurry's base damage, or whether they are additive with other damage buckets. If they are additive, this build is dead on arrival and you should stop. Second, this build has no answer yet to the thing that makes the bow builds work - Eaglehorn's wall bounce has no melee equivalent - so measure single-target damage against a lair boss, not clear speed on trash, because clear speed is where melee will look artificially fine. Third, the honest counter-evidence: Maxroll rates Flurry B tier on BOTH its overall endgame list (updated 2026-07-22) and its push list (updated 2026-07-23), and both dates are after the 3.1.0 buffs landed. If experienced theorycrafters already looked at these numbers and left Flurry in B, the most likely explanation is that they are right and I am wrong. Treat a successful test as surprising. Fourth, survivability: you are in melee range without Harlequin Crest's old Damage Reduction, which was removed in 3.1.x - if you are dying more than once per Pit run at your farming tier, the build is not viable regardless of its damage.
